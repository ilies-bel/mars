# UI topology drill-in is anchored expand plus local nudge, not native relayout; supersedes ADR-0043's drill-in clause

ADR-0043 adopted @antv/g6 for the ui/ topology graph and decided drill-in would be an in-place mode swap (Cloud force-cloud -> a separate Subgraph antv-dagre of one Proposal's Focus subgraph), 'not nested combo expansion, which sidesteps comboCombined's inability to re-run the inner dagre on expand'. That drill-in clause is wrong on the facts and is superseded here. ADR-0043's other decisions stand: G6 is adopted, the zero-dependency pure-SVG viewer ends, an SVG renderer remains available as a fallback.

Corrected facts (verified in the throwaway prototype at /tmp/mars-graph-proto): nested combo expansion IS workable. G6's native collapse-expand reveals a cluster's child tasks but does not lay them out (they stack at the canvas origin); a relayout DOES lay them out via the inner dagre. The real blocker is different: comboCombined's relayout re-runs the OUTER force over every cluster, relocating all clusters by up to ~600px, and it ignores any pin on the expanding cluster (fixed combo coordinates are not honoured in G6 v5.1.1 — attempted via updateComboData fx/fy and via the layout's fixed handling, both failed). So a relayout can lay the children out but can never keep the opened subgraph where the operator was looking.

Decision: drill-in is ANCHORED EXPAND + LOCAL NUDGE on a SINGLE graph, and the native relayout (graph.layout()) is NOT called on a toggle. The native collapse-expand behavior is kept only for the double-click trigger and the reveal/hide of child tasks. On expand: (1) read the cluster's resting position as the anchor (native expand zeroes the live position, so the resting position is tracked separately); (2) lay the child tasks out as a deterministic crossing-reduced LR layered graph centred on that anchor — the cluster opens exactly where its card sat and the card never jumps; (3) displace only the clusters whose footprint the opened subgraph overlaps (a bounded local nudge — 0-3 neighbours, tens of pixels — not a global reflow); every other cluster stays fixed. Collapse hides the children natively and slides the nudged neighbours home. Any number of clusters may be open at once, each anchored at its own card. The opened subgraph is not auto-fitted or auto-zoomed: a large cluster may extend past the viewport and the operator pans to it; the camera never moves on its own.

Trade-offs: this re-introduces a hand-rolled subgraph layout and a hand-rolled neighbour-displacement pass instead of leaning on the library's relayout — more code than 'just call graph.layout()', and the nudge geometry needs tuning. We accept that because the alternatives are worse: native relayout reshuffles the entire cloud on every toggle and cannot anchor (fails the core requirement that a cluster opens where the operator is looking and keeps their place), and the ADR-0043 mode-swap needs a second graph plus a Cloud/Subgraph visibility swap and loses the single-canvas continuity. Anchored expand keeps one canvas, keeps spatial stability, and is the only option that puts the subgraph at the parent. This decision supersedes the drill-in clause of ADR-0043 only.
